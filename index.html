Depth top — facing to back wall at the top of the opening. The gap against the floor depth is back-wall slope, which is often demolishable.
Hearth — non-combustible hearth out from the facing. Most models need 16".
Rear width — the back of the firebox. Feet are usually the widest part of the stove and are reported separately.
Lintel — thickness front to back. An offset adaptor is flagged only when the lintel reaches past the flue collar and under 6" clears above the stove.

Room to work. The per-side figure is the gap between your opening and the stove body. Manufacturers design in about ½" a side; more is easier, up to the point where the surround can no longer cover the opening, which is flagged separately.

Feet are reported separately because they are small points rather than a continuous face — where they are the tight spot, a channel cut for the feet is often enough, which is a far smaller job than opening up a back wall.

The manufacturer's published minimum comes first. Every fit decision here is tested against the figure in the manual or spec sheet. Body dimensions, the brick allowance, feet widths and squeeze heights are wiggle room — identified separately, shown as flags, never used to loosen the published minimum.

Height and width at the facing are hard cutoffs. Nothing can be gained at the face, so a model short on either is ruled out.

The 4" allowance applies to the rear width and to depth only. Fireboxes taper inward, so the rear is the pinch point, and removing or reorienting brick at the back or sides can buy room there. A model short on rear width or depth by up to 4" is flagged for inspection rather than excluded.

Surround coverage. If the opening is bigger than the largest available surround, the model is flagged, not excluded — raising the firebox floor, refacing, or a custom surround can still make it work.

Manufacturers warn against removing brick or mortar from a masonry fireplace. Regency and MF Fire both state it explicitly. These flags mark a job for your inspection and judgement, not a green light.

BTU is the EPA-tested figure where published, with the advertised maximum struck through.

Rear widths are unverified. They were read off unlabelled dimension drawings and have not been confirmed against a unit. Treat them as a prompt to check, never as a pass. Regency publishes no plan view at all, so no rear width exists for those models.
