Insert fit finder

Measure the fireplace opening at the facing material.

Opening at facing

Facing to back wall

Bottom is at the firebox floor, top is at the top of the opening. The gap between them is back-wall slope.

Lintel thickness

Front to back through the lintel itself. One brick — about 4" — is typical. Deeper lintels can force the offset flue adaptor.

Hearth in front of the facing

Non-combustible hearth measured out from the facing. Most models need 16"; a few need 18–20".

Rear firebox width — optional

Tapered fireboxes narrow toward the back; the insert body still has to pass.

The facing dimensions are hard cutoffs. Height, width and depth measured at the facing rule a model out with no allowance — nothing can be gained at the face.

The 4" allowance applies at the rear only. Fireboxes taper inward, so the rear is the pinch point, and removing or reorienting sidewall brick can buy room there. A model short at the rear by up to 4" is flagged for inspection rather than excluded.

Surround coverage. If the opening is bigger than the largest available surround, the model is flagged, not excluded — raising the firebox floor, refacing, or a custom surround can still make it work.

Manufacturers warn against removing brick or mortar from a masonry fireplace. Regency and MF Fire both state it explicitly. These flags mark a job for your inspection and judgement, not a green light.

BTU is the EPA-tested figure where published, advertised struck through. Tax credit = US Biomass Rebate, needs HHV ≥ 75%.

Rear widths are unverified. They were read off unlabelled dimension drawings and have not been confirmed against a unit. Treat them as a prompt to check, never as a pass. Regency publishes no plan view at all, so no rear width exists for those models.
